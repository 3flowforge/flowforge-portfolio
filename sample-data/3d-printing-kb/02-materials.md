# Materials

## PLA

PLA is the cheapest and easiest FDM material. Good for prototypes, display models and fixtures that stay indoors. It is stiff but brittle under impact and starts to soften around 55 to 60 C, so it is not suitable for hot cars, dishwashers or outdoor sun. Colors: black, white, grey, red, blue.

## PETG

PETG (FDM) is tougher than PLA, less brittle, more chemical resistant and handles heat up to about 70 to 80 C. Good for functional brackets, enclosures and snap-fit parts. Slightly glossy surface. Not certified food safe as printed.

## ABS and ASA

ABS and ASA (FDM) handle heat up to about 90 to 100 C and can be vapor smoothed for a glossy finish. ASA is UV resistant and is our recommendation for outdoor parts. Both can warp on large flat parts; we print them in an enclosed printer.

## TPU

TPU 95A (FDM) is a flexible, rubber-like material for gaskets, bumpers, phone cases and grips. Walls thinner than 1.2 mm will feel very soft. Fine details and sharp overhangs are harder to print in TPU.

## Resin (SLA)

Standard resin (SLA) gives very fine detail (layer height down to 0.025 mm) and a smooth surface, ideal for miniatures, jewelry masters and visual prototypes. It is brittle and can yellow in sunlight. Tough resin is more impact resistant and is used for functional prototypes and snap fits. All resin parts are washed and UV post-cured.

## Nylon PA12 (SLS)

Nylon PA12 (SLS) is our strongest general-purpose material: tough, slightly flexible in thin sections, chemically resistant, with a matte grainy surface. No support structures are needed, so complex and interlocking parts can be printed in one piece. Natural color is grey-white; black dyeing is available.

## Choosing a material

Quick guide: cheapest prototype = PLA; functional indoor part = PETG; outdoor or hot environment = ASA; flexible = TPU; finest detail or smooth surface = standard resin; functional resin prototype = tough resin; strongest part or many small parts = Nylon PA12 (SLS).
